Whether calibrating deep neural network uncertainties, formulating loss objectives under maximum likelihood principles, or bounding sample errors in high-dimensional empirical risk minimization, a rigorous theoretical grounding is indispensable.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Academic Anchor</strong>: Théorie analytique des probabilités (Pierre-Simon Laplace, 1812). This seminal publication laid the theoretical bedrock for Laplace (Double Exponential) Distribution, proving the core theorems and analytical invariants that modern data scientists and statistical learning theorists rely upon.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>The objective of this study note is to establish the complete analytical machinery of Laplace (Double Exponential) Distribution. We bypass hand-waving heuristics to derive the underlying equations from first principles, examine structural symmetries, and verify empirical behavior through high-precision computational experiments.</p>
