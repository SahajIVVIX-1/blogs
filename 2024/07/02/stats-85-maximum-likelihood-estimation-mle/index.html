1. Academic Anchor & Foundational Context

In the formal progression of mathematical statistics and machine learning, Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) occupies a foundational role within Statistical Estimation & Inference. Whether calibrating deep neural network uncertainties, formulating loss objectives under maximum likelihood principles, or bounding sample errors in high-dimensional empirical risk minimization, a rigorous theoretical grounding is indispensable.

Academic Anchor: On the Mathematical Foundations of Theoretical Statistics (Ronald A. Fisher, 1922). This seminal publication laid the theoretical bedrock for Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE), proving the core theorems and analytical invariants that modern data scientists and statistical learning theorists rely upon.

The objective of this study note is to establish the complete analytical machinery of Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE). We bypass hand-waving heuristics to derive the underlying equations from first principles, examine structural symmetries, and verify empirical behavior through high-precision computational experiments.

3. First-Principles Mathematical Derivations

3.1. Formal Analytical Grounding

Let (Ω,F,P)(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)(Ω,F,P) denote a complete probability space, where Ω\OmegaΩ is the sample space of elementary outcomes, F\mathcal{F}F is the associated Borel σ\sigmaσ-algebra of events, and P:F→[0,1]P: \mathcal{F} \to [0, 1]P:F→[0,1] represents the normalized probability measure.

Within the context of Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE), consider a random variable or parameter vector X:Ω→RdX: \Omega \to \mathbb{R}^dX:Ω→Rd governed by the parametric family {Pθ:θ∈Θ⊆Rp}\{P_\theta : \theta \in \Theta \subseteq \mathbb{R}^p\}{Pθ​:θ∈Θ⊆Rp}. The foundational governing formulation is specified by:

L(θ;x)=∏i=1nf(xi;θ)  ⟹  ℓ(θ;x)=∑i=1nln⁡f(xi;θ)\mathcal{L}(\theta; x) = \prod_{i=1}^n f(x_i; \theta) \quad \implies \quad \ell(\theta; x) = \sum_{i=1}^n \ln f(x_i; \theta)L(θ;x)=∏i=1n​f(xi​;θ)⟹ℓ(θ;x)=∑i=1n​lnf(xi​;θ)

When evaluating expectation functionals under measure PθP_\thetaPθ​, the first and second-order operators satisfy the linear integral identities:

Eθ[g(X)]=∫Ωg(x) dPθ(x)=∫Rdg(x)f(x;θ) dx\mathbb{E}_\theta[g(X)] = \int_\Omega g(x) \, dP_\theta(x) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^d} g(x) f(x; \theta) \, dxEθ​[g(X)]=∫Ω​g(x)dPθ​(x)=∫Rd​g(x)f(x;θ)dx

Var⁡θ(g(X))=Eθ[(g(X)−Eθ[g(X)])2]=Eθ[g(X)2]−(Eθ[g(X)])2\operatorname{Var}_\theta(g(X)) = \mathbb{E}_\theta\left[(g(X) - \mathbb{E}_\theta[g(X)])^2\right] = \mathbb{E}_\theta[g(X)^2] - (\mathbb{E}_\theta[g(X)])^2Varθ​(g(X))=Eθ​[(g(X)−Eθ​[g(X)])2]=Eθ​[g(X)2]−(Eθ​[g(X)])2

3.2. Asymptotic Bounds & Structural Invariants

Applying the fundamental theorems of Statistical Estimation & Inference, we establish the primary asymptotic bounds. In particular, under standard regularity conditions (differentiability of the density f(x;θ)f(x; \theta)f(x;θ) with respect to θ\thetaθ and support independent of θ\thetaθ), the score function S(θ)S(\theta)S(θ) and Fisher Information Matrix I(θ)\mathcal{I}(\theta)I(θ) emerge naturally:

S(θ)=∇θln⁡f(X;θ),E[S(θ)]=0S(\theta) = \nabla_\theta \ln f(X; \theta), \quad \mathbb{E}[S(\theta)] = \mathbf{0}S(θ)=∇θ​lnf(X;θ),E[S(θ)]=0

I(θ)=E[S(θ)S(θ)T]=−E[∇θ2ln⁡f(X;θ)]\mathcal{I}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\left[ S(\theta) S(\theta)^T \right] = -\mathbb{E}\left[ \nabla_\theta^2 \ln f(X; \theta) \right]I(θ)=E[S(θ)S(θ)T]=−E[∇θ2​lnf(X;θ)]

The Cramér-Rao regularity conditions dictate that for any unbiased estimator θ^(X)\hat{\theta}(X)θ^(X) of θ\thetaθ, the covariance matrix satisfies the matrix partial order:

Cov⁡(θ^)⪰1nI(θ)−1\operatorname{Cov}(\hat{\theta}) \succeq \frac{1}{n} \mathcal{I}(\theta)^{-1}Cov(θ^)⪰n1​I(θ)−1

This matrix inequality establishes the fundamental efficiency ceiling for any unbiased estimation protocol under Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE).

5. Numerical Stability, Edge Cases & Failure Modes

When moving from pure blackboard mathematics to production statistical software, practitioners must navigate several critical numerical hazards associated with Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE):

  1. Catastrophic Cancellation in Variance Calculations: Computing sample variance via the naive algebraic expansion ∑xi2−1n(∑xi)2\sum x_i^2 - \frac{1}{n}(\sum x_i)^2∑xi2​−n1​(∑xi​)2 suffers from devastating floating-point cancellation when σ2≪μ2\sigma^2 \ll \mu^2σ2≪μ2. Production libraries must implement Welford's one-pass algorithm or two-pass compensated summation.
  2. Log-Sum-Exp & Floating-Point Underflow: Evaluating joint likelihoods across large sample sizes requires strictly operating in log-probability space ℓ(θ)=∑ln⁡f(xi)\ell(\theta) = \sum \ln f(x_i)ℓ(θ)=∑lnf(xi​). When computing normalizing constants or mixture weights, utilize the numerically stable Log-Sum-Exp identity: ln⁡∑k=1Kezk=m+ln⁡∑k=1Kezk−m,where m=max⁡kzk\ln \sum_{k=1}^K e^{z_k} = m + \ln \sum_{k=1}^K e^{z_k - m}, \quad \text{where } m = \max_k z_kln∑k=1K​ezk​=m+ln∑k=1K​ezk​−m,where m=maxk​zk​
  3. Boundary Parameter Singularities & Support Violations: When parameter values approach boundary conditions (e.g., variance σ2→0\sigma^2 \to 0σ2→0, degrees of freedom ν→0\nu \to 0ν→0, or correlation ∣r∣→1|r| \to 1∣r∣→1), information matrices become severely ill-conditioned. Regularization via weakly informative priors (e.g., half-Cauchy or LKJ correlation priors) or Fisher scoring damping is essential.
