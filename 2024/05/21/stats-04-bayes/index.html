Probability and Statistics · Bayes' theorem

Bayes' theorem as updating a prior with evidence, the base-rate trap in medical tests, and sequential updating.

$$P(H \mid E) = \frac{P(E \mid H),P(H)}{P(E)}$$

  • Prior P(H): what I believed before seeing the evidence.
  • Likelihood P(E | H): how expected the evidence is if H is true.
  • Posterior P(H | E): the updated belief.
  • P(E) comes from the law of total probability (last post).

The base-rate trap. Take a disease with 1% prevalence and a test with 95% sensitivity and 95% specificity. If you test positive, the chance you have the disease is far below 95%. The base rate dominates, because there are so many more healthy people who can produce false positives.

Updating is also sequential: today’s posterior becomes tomorrow’s prior.

def posterior(prior, sens, spec):
    p_pos = sens * prior + (1 - spec) * (1 - prior)
    return sens * prior / p_pos

p = posterior(prior=0.01, sens=0.95, spec=0.95)
print("P(disease | 1 positive) =", round(p, 4))
p2 = posterior(prior=p, sens=0.95, spec=0.95)
print("P(disease | 2 positives) =", round(p2, 4))

# counting view with 10,000 people
n = 10_000
sick = int(n * 0.01)
tp = round(sick * 0.95)
fp = round((n - sick) * 0.05)
print(f"true positives {tp}, false positives {fp}, share sick among positives {tp / (tp + fp):.4f}")

Output:

P(disease | 1 positive) = 0.161
P(disease | 2 positives) = 0.7848
true positives 95, false positives 495, share sick among positives 0.1610
