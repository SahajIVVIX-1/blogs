1f:T6a2,<p>On 28 March 2026 I committed <strong>Modern-DCGAN-Reproducibility</strong>, a study of the DCGAN paper (Radford et al., 2015), implemented in PyTorch with Jupyter notebooks.</p>
<h2 id="what-changed-from-the-paper">What changed from the paper</h2>
<p>The README compares the paper's choices with this implementation:</p>



































<table><thead><tr><th>component</th><th>2015 paper</th><th>this repo</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>loss</td><td>binary cross-entropy</td><td><strong>LSGAN</strong> (least squares)</td></tr><tr><td>discriminator normalisation</td><td>batch norm</td><td><strong>spectral normalisation</strong></td></tr><tr><td>initialisation</td><td>Normal(0, 0.02)</td><td>Xavier/Kaiming hybrid</td></tr><tr><td>regularisation</td><td>none</td><td>discriminator dropout (0.3)</td></tr><tr><td>labels</td><td>hard 0/1</td><td><strong>label smoothing</strong> (0.1/0.9)</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>The all-convolutional generator and discriminator from the paper are kept.</p>
<h2 id="analysis-layer">Analysis layer</h2>
<p>A <code>GANAnalyzer</code> class uses PyTorch hooks to:</p>
<ul>
<li>plot the RGB filters of the discriminator's first layer, after a 25-epoch CIFAR-10 run</li>
<li>show mid-layer generator feature maps (e.g. at 16×16)</li>
<li>draw histograms of D(x) versus D(G(z)), to watch for mode collapse</li>
</ul>
<p>It also produces a 4-panel training dashboard.</p>
