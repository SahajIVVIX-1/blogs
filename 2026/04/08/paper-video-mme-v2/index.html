Paper summary: Video-MME-v2: Towards the Next Stage in Benchmarks for Comprehensive Video Understanding

Video-MME-v2 is a harder, human-built video understanding benchmark with a three-level hierarchy and group-based scoring that only credits consistent, well-reasoned answers; the best model, Gemini-3-Pro, still trails human experts substantially.

Paper card
Paper
Video-MME-v2: Towards the Next Stage in Benchmarks for Comprehensive Video Understanding (2026)
Authors
Chaoyou Fu, Haozhi Yuan, Yuhao Dong, Yi-Fan Zhang, Yunhang Shen, Xiaoxing Hu et al.
Task
Robust, faithful evaluation of video understanding
Method
Three-level hierarchy (information aggregation → temporal dynamics → multimodal reasoning); group-based non-linear scoring
Data
Human-annotated: 12 annotators, 50 reviewers, 3,300 human-hours, up to 5 QA rounds
Result
Large gap between Gemini-3-Pro and human experts, per the abstract
Links
arXiv · PDF

Problem

Video benchmarks are saturating: leaderboard scores look high while real abilities lag behind.

Method

Video-MME-v2 uses:

  • a progressive three-level hierarchy: multi-point visual information aggregation, then temporal dynamics, then complex multimodal reasoning
  • group-based non-linear evaluation: credit requires consistency across related questions and coherent multi-step reasoning, so fragmented or guessed correctness is penalised
  • a controlled human annotation pipeline: 12 annotators, 50 reviewers, 3,300 human-hours, up to 5 rounds of quality assurance

Key results

From the abstract: a substantial gap between the best current model, Gemini-3-Pro, and human experts, with a clear hierarchy in difficulty across the levels.

Limitations

There’s no dedicated limitations section in the extracted text. Human-built benchmarks are expensive to extend.
