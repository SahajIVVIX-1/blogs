Paper summary: rStar-Math: Small LLMs Can Master Math Reasoning with Self-Evolved Deep Thinking

rStar-Math gets small language models to strong maths reasoning with Monte Carlo Tree Search guided by a small process preference model, both improved through self-evolution without distillation from stronger models.

Paper card
Paper
rStar-Math: Small LLMs Can Master Math Reasoning with Self-Evolved Deep Thinking (2025)
Authors
Xinyu Guan, Li Lyna Zhang, Yifei Liu, Ning Shang, Youran Sun, Yi Zhu et al.
Task
Maths reasoning with small language models
Method
MCTS test-time search; code-augmented CoT data synthesis; process preference model (PPM); four rounds of self-evolution
Data
Maths problems; trajectories generated by MCTS rollouts
Result
MATH: Qwen2.5-Math-7B 58.8%→90.0%, Phi3-mini 41.4%→86.4% (above o1-preview); AIME 53.3%, per the abstract
Links
arXiv · PDF

Problem

Strong maths reasoning seemed to need very large models, or distillation from them. Can small language models get there on their own?

Method

rStar-Math has a policy SLM search step by step through solutions with Monte Carlo Tree Search, guided by an SLM-based process reward model. Three ideas make the two small models trainable:

  1. Code-augmented CoT synthesis: extensive MCTS rollouts generate step-by-step reasoning in which each step includes code that can be executed, giving verified trajectories for training the policy.
  2. A process preference model (PPM), trained without naive per-step score labels.
  3. Self-evolution: policy and PPM are built from scratch and improved over several rounds.

Key results

From the abstract, after 4 rounds of self-evolution with millions of synthesised solutions for 747k problems:

  • MATH: Qwen2.5-Math-7B goes from 58.8% to 90.0%, and Phi3-mini-3.8B from 41.4% to 86.4%, above o1-preview by 4.5 and 0.9 points
  • AIME: an average of 53.3% (8 of 15) problems solved

All of this without distillation from a superior model.

Limitations

There’s no dedicated limitations section in the extracted text. MCTS at test time means many model calls per problem, so the approach trades inference compute for accuracy.
