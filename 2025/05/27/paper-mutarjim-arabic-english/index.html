Paper summary: Mutarjim: Advancing Bidirectional Arabic-English Translation with a Small Language Model

Mutarjim is a 1.5B-parameter Arabic–English translation model that's reported to rival models up to 20× larger, plus Tarjama-25, a new 5,000-pair benchmark built to fix gaps in existing test sets.

📄 RESEARCH PAPER CARD
Paper
Mutarjim: Advancing Bidirectional Arabic-English Translation with a Small Language Model (2025)
Authors
Khalil Hennara, Muhammad Hreden, Mohamed Motaism Hamed, Zeina Aldallal, Sara Chrouf, Safwan AlModhayan
Task
Bidirectional Arabic–English machine translation
Method
Two-phase training of Kuwain-1.5B on a curated high-quality corpus
Dataset
Established benchmarks plus Tarjama-25 (5,000 expert-reviewed pairs)
Key Result
Rivals models up to 20× larger; SOTA on Tarjama-25 English→Arabic, above GPT-4o mini, per the abstract
Links
arXiv Page ↗PDF Full Text ↗

Problem

Large LLMs translate well but are expensive. Can a small, specialised model compete for Arabic–English? Existing benchmarks also have issues: narrow domains, short sentences and an English-source bias.

Method

Mutarjim builds on Kuwain-1.5B, a model tailored for Arabic and English, with an optimised two-phase training approach and a carefully curated corpus. The authors also introduce Tarjama-25, 5,000 expert-reviewed sentence pairs across a wide range of domains.

Key results

From the abstract: Mutarjim outperforms much larger models on several established benchmarks and rivals models up to 20× larger at much lower cost. On Tarjama-25 it reaches state of the art for English→Arabic, surpassing larger and proprietary models such as GPT-4o mini.

Limitations

There’s no dedicated limitations section in the extracted text. The headline English→Arabic result is on the authors’ own new benchmark.
