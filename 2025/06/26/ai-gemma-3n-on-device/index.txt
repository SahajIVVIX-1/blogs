1d:T4cd,<h2 id="what-happened">What happened</h2>
<p><strong>Gemma 3n</strong>, previewed at Google I/O, became available across the main open-source tools: Transformers, timm, MLX, llama.cpp (text), transformers.js, Ollama and Google AI Edge.</p>
<h2 id="the-technical-details">The technical details</h2>
<p>From the post:</p>
<ul>
<li>two sizes, <strong><code>gemma-3n-E2B</code></strong> and <strong><code>gemma-3n-E4B</code></strong> (base and instruct). <strong>E</strong> stands for <strong>effective</strong>:
<ul>
<li>real parameter counts are <strong>5B</strong> and <strong>8B</strong></li>
<li>thanks to memory-efficiency techniques, they need only about <strong>2B / 4B</strong> of VRAM, and run in about <strong>2GB</strong> and <strong>3GB</strong> of GPU RAM</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>natively multimodal: <strong>image, text, audio and video</strong> inputs</li>
<li>besides the language decoder, an <strong>audio encoder</strong> and a <strong>MobileNet-V5</strong> vision encoder</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="why-it-matters">Why it matters</h2>
<p>Parameter count and memory use are no longer the same thing.
