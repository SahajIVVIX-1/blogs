Paper summary: Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning in Large Language Models with One Training Example

RL with verifiable rewards on a single maths training example lifts Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B on MATH500 from 36.0% to 73.6%, matching training on a 1.2k-example dataset.

Paper card
Paper
Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning in Large Language Models with One Training Example (2025)
Authors
Yiping Wang, Qing Yang, Zhiyuan Zeng, Liliang Ren, Liyuan Liu, Baolin Peng et al.
Task
Data efficiency of RL for maths reasoning
Method
RLVR with one (or two) training examples
Data
Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and other models; MATH500 and five other maths benchmarks
Result
MATH500 36.0% → 73.6% with one example (8.6% beyond format correction), matching the 1.2k DeepScaleR subset, per the abstract
Links
arXiv · PDF · code

Problem

How much data does RL with verifiable rewards actually need to improve maths reasoning?

Method

Apply RLVR to a base model with just one training example (“1-shot RLVR”), and compare with the same training on a larger dataset that contains that example.

Key results

From the abstract, on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B:

  • MATH500: 36.0% → 73.6% with one example, of which 8.6 points go beyond format correction
  • average across six maths benchmarks: 17.6% → 35.7% (7.0 points non-format gain)
  • this matches training on the 1.2k-example DeepScaleR subset (73.6%, 35.9%)
  • two examples slightly exceed it (74.8%, 36.6%)
  • similar gains appear for Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Llama3.2-3B-Instruct and others

Limitations

From the paper: larger models such as Qwen2.5-32B weren’t tried because of compute limits, and only the maths domain was studied, not coding or other verifiable domains.
