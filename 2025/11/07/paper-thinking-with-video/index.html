Paper summary: Thinking with Video: Video Generation as a Promising Multimodal Reasoning Paradigm

"Thinking with Video" uses a video generation model (Sora-2) as a reasoner, drawing its solution as video frames; on a new benchmark it rivals strong VLMs on visual puzzles and reaches 92% on MATH.

📄 RESEARCH PAPER CARD
Paper
Thinking with Video: Video Generation as a Promising Multimodal Reasoning Paradigm (2025)
Authors
Jingqi Tong, Yurong Mou, Hangcheng Li, Mingzhe Li, Yongzhuo Yang, Ming Zhang et al.
Task
Using video generation as a unified multimodal reasoning medium
Method
Prompt a video generation model (Sora-2) to solve tasks in generated frames; VideoThinkBench
Dataset
VideoThinkBench: vision-centric (e.g. eyeballing puzzles) and text-centric (e.g. GSM8K, MMMU) tasks
Key Result
Comparable to SOTA VLMs on vision tasks, +10% over GPT-5 on eyeballing puzzles; 92% on MATH, 69.2% on MMMU, per the abstract
Links
arXiv Page ↗PDF Full Text ↗Code Repository ↗

Problem

“Thinking with text” (chain-of-thought) and “thinking with images” both help models reason, but according to the abstract, images capture only single moments, and text and vision stay separate modalities.

Method

The authors propose “Thinking with Video”: use a video generation model such as Sora-2 as the reasoner, with video frames as a unified medium for working through a problem. They build VideoThinkBench, with vision-centric tasks (like eyeballing puzzles) and text-centric tasks (like GSM8K and MMMU). Figure 1 shows example solutions.

Key results

From the abstract:

  • vision-centric tasks: comparable to state-of-the-art VLMs, and 10% above GPT-5 on eyeballing puzzles
  • text-centric tasks: 92% on MATH and 69.2% on MMMU
  • self-consistency and in-context learning improve Sora-2’s performance further

Limitations

From the paper: evaluation mainly covers Sora-2 among video models, and since Sora-2 isn’t open-source, its internal mechanisms can’t be analysed. More (especially open) video models are planned.
