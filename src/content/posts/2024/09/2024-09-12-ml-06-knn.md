k-NN is the simplest non-parametric model. To predict, find the **k closest training points** and take a majority vote (for regression, the average).

- There's no real training step. All the work happens at prediction time, so it's slow on big data.
- **Distance is everything.** If one feature is measured in thousands and another in fractions, the big one dominates. Always **scale** first.
- **k** controls flexibility. k = 1 memorises the training set (high variance). A large k smooths everything out (high bias).

I swept k on the wine dataset, with and without scaling.

```python run
from sklearn.datasets import load_wine
from sklearn.model_selection import cross_val_score
from sklearn.neighbors import KNeighborsClassifier
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler
from sklearn.pipeline import make_pipeline

X, y = load_wine(return_X_y=True)
print("feature ranges differ a lot:", X.min(0).round(1)[:4], "...", X.max(0).round(1)[-2:])

for k in [1, 5, 15, 51]:
    raw = cross_val_score(KNeighborsClassifier(k), X, y, cv=5).mean()
    scaled = cross_val_score(make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), KNeighborsClassifier(k)), X, y, cv=5).mean()
    print(f"k={k:>2}: unscaled {raw:.3f} | scaled {scaled:.3f}")
```
